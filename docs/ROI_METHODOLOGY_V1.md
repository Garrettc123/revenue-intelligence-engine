# ROI Methodology v1

## Purpose

This methodology provides a reproducible way to measure the financial effect of an RHNS-supported workflow. It does not establish guaranteed, typical, or future results.

## Required measurement charter

Before measurement begins, record:

- Client legal name and authorized reviewer.
- Engagement name and scope.
- Baseline and intervention periods.
- Primary operational metric.
- Financial metric.
- Source systems and source owners.
- Direct program cost.
- Margin source if profit is to be reported.
- Attribution tier.
- Known limitations.

## Financial definitions

```text
Attributed incremental revenue:
Revenue estimated to be above a documented comparator and attributable only to the degree supported by the selected attribution tier.

Attributed incremental gross profit:
Attributed incremental revenue multiplied by a documented gross-margin rate or job-level gross-profit record.

Net contribution:
Attributed incremental gross profit minus direct program cost.

ROI:
Net contribution divided by direct program cost, expressed as a percentage.
```

$$
\text{Attributed Incremental Gross Profit}
=
\text{Attributed Incremental Revenue}
\times
\text{Documented Gross Margin}
$$

$$
\text{Net Contribution}
=
\text{Attributed Incremental Gross Profit}
-
\text{Direct Program Cost}
$$

$$
\text{ROI}
=
\frac{\text{Net Contribution}}{\text{Direct Program Cost}}
\times 100
$$

$$
\text{Payback Months}
=
\frac{\text{Direct Program Cost}}{\text{Average Monthly Attributed Incremental Gross Profit}}
$$

## Attribution tiers

| Tier | Method | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No comparator | Observed activity only |
| 1 | Linked workflow/action trace | Traceable association |
| 2 | Historical pre/post baseline | Estimated improvement with limitations |
| 3 | Matched cohort or comparable segment | Stronger estimated improvement |
| 4 | Credible treatment/control comparison | Estimated incremental impact |

## Claim restrictions

- Do not calculate ROI without a positive direct program cost.
- Do not call revenue "profit" without a documented margin source.
- Do not use "incremental" without an attribution tier of 2 or higher.
- Do not use "verified" without reconciled source evidence and required sign-off.
- Do not use "typical" without a representative dataset and documented analysis.
- Do not publish results without client permission and approved wording.

## Limitation language

Every financial result must disclose its measurement period, source inputs, attribution tier, assumptions, and material limitations such as seasonality, lead-quality shifts, pricing changes, staffing changes, delivery constraints, incomplete records, and market conditions.
