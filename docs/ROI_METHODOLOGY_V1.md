# ROI Methodology v1

## Purpose

This methodology measures the financial effect of an RHNS-supported workflow. It does not establish guaranteed, typical, or future results.

## Measurement charter

Before measurement begins, record the client, engagement scope, baseline and intervention periods, primary and financial metrics, source systems, direct program cost, margin source, attribution tier, known limitations, and authorized reviewer.

## Definitions

- **Attributed incremental revenue**: revenue estimated above a documented comparator, attributable only to the degree supported by the selected attribution tier.
- **Attributed incremental gross profit**: attributed incremental revenue multiplied by a documented gross-margin rate or job-level gross-profit record.
- **Net contribution**: attributed incremental gross profit minus direct program cost.
- **ROI**: net contribution divided by direct program cost, expressed as a percentage.

```text
Gross profit = attributed incremental revenue x documented gross margin
Net contribution = gross profit - direct program cost
ROI = net contribution / direct program cost x 100
Payback months = direct program cost / monthly attributed incremental gross profit
```

## Attribution tiers

- Tier 0: no comparator; observed activity only.
- Tier 1: linked workflow/action trace; traceable association.
- Tier 2: historical pre/post baseline; estimated improvement with limitations.
- Tier 3: matched cohort or comparable segment; stronger estimated improvement.
- Tier 4: credible treatment/control comparison; estimated incremental impact.

## Claim restrictions

- Do not calculate ROI without positive direct program cost.
- Do not call revenue profit without a documented margin source.
- Do not use incremental language without attribution tier 2 or higher.
- Do not use verified without reconciled evidence and required sign-off.
- Do not use typical without representative evidence and documented analysis.
- Do not publish results without client permission and approved wording.

Every result must disclose its period, inputs, attribution tier, assumptions, and material limitations.
