# Behavioral Integrity Policy v1

## Principle

RHNS may help buyers make informed decisions. It must not use deception, coercion, concealment, or exploitation to force a commercial outcome.

## Allowed behavior

- Summarize verified business context.
- Identify a stated or observable decision barrier.
- Draft plain-language explanations of scope, price, assumptions, trade-offs, implementation, and risk.
- Offer a voluntary next step and an easy decline or opt-out path.
- Prepare factual comparison, ROI, implementation, or decision documents.

## Prohibited behavior

- Fabricated scarcity, deadlines, availability, endorsements, or testimonials.
- False or unsubstantiated revenue, earning, savings, conversion, or ROI claims.
- Concealing material price, renewal, cancellation, scope, risk, or data-use terms.
- Impersonation or undisclosed automated identity.
- Repeated outreach after an opt-out or clear request to stop.
- Exploitation of sensitive traits, vulnerability, or distress.
- Pressure tactics designed to prevent informed refusal.
- Personalization based on undisclosed sensitive information.

## Required message properties

External commercial drafts must include:

- Accurate sender identity.
- Clear commercial purpose where applicable.
- Claims supportable by linked evidence.
- Material limitations and conditions where relevant.
- A respectful, practical way to decline or stop contact.

## Buyer-state rule

Buyer states are hypotheses, not diagnoses. Every hypothesis must list observed evidence, confidence, and at least one plausible alternative explanation.

## Escalation

Block and escalate a draft when it includes a financial claim, legal statement, price or scope commitment, sensitive-data inference, high-pressure language, or conflict with an opt-out/consent record.
